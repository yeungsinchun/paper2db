6. Read the following description about a mirage and answer the questions that follow.
A mirage is often seen on highways during hot summers. Pools of water seem to cover the
roadway far ahead. Distant objects appear to be reflected by the surface of the ‘water’. The
phenomenon is caused by the difference in refractive index between the hot air near the road
surface and the cooler air above it. The refractive index of cool air is greater than that of hot air,
but the differences are so small that the subsequent deviations of light rays are tiny. Sufficiently
large temperature differences between the hot air near the road surface and the above cooler air
over a short height (i.e. high temperature gradient) and light rays travelling along sufficiently long
path lengths are required to form a mirage.
