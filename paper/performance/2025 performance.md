# Candidates' Performance

## Paper 1

Paper 1 consists of two sections: multiple-choice questions in Section A and conventional questions in Section B. All questions in both sections are compulsory.

### Section A (multiple-choice questions)

Section A consisted of 33 multiple-choice questions and the mean score was 19. Post-examination analysis revealed the following:

#### Question 1

An equal amount of energy is supplied to each of the substances *P*, *Q*, *R* and *S* of mass and specific heat capacity listed below. Which substance would have the largest rise in temperature?

| | substance | mass / kg | specific heat capacity / J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹ | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. | P | 2 | 4200 | (6%) |
| B. | Q | 3 | 2100 | (1%) |
| *C. | R | 4 | 900 | (88%) |
| D. | S | 5 | 840 | (5%) |

Most candidates correctly applied the relation Δ*T* = *Q* / (*mc*), identifying that substance *R*, having the smallest product of mass and specific heat capacity, would undergo the largest temperature rise.

#### Question 3

Identical closed vessels *X* and *Y* contain carbon dioxide (CO₂) and oxygen (O₂) respectively.

*[diagram omitted]*

Both gases are at room temperature and atmospheric pressure. Which of the following physical quantities of the gas molecules in the two vessels is/are the same?

(1) root-mean-square speed
(2) average kinetic energy
(3) frequency of collision with the walls of the vessel

- A. (1) only (11%)
- *B. (2) only (33%)
- C. (1) and (3) only (13%)
- D. (2) and (3) only (43%)

About half of the candidates did not realise that the frequency of collision of the molecules with the walls of the vessel should be different for the two gases.

#### Question 9

*[diagram omitted]*

*X* and *Y* are two points on an incline making an angle θ with the horizontal. A particle projected horizontally from *X* with speed *u* lands at *Y* as shown. Which expression correctly gives its time of flight from *X* to *Y*? Neglect air resistance.

- A. (u sinθ)/(2g) (15%)
- B. (u tanθ)/(2g) (19%)
- C. (2u sinθ)/g (18%)
- *D. (2u tanθ)/g (48%)

Nearly half of the candidates were able to apply the equations of projectile motion and to carry out multi-step calculations to arrive at the correct answer.

#### Question 12

*[diagram omitted]*

Two identical objects located at point *X* and point *Y* are at distances *r* and 2*r* from the Earth's centre. The weight of the object at point *X* is *W*. What is the weight of the object at point *Y*?

- *A. 0.25W (77%)
- B. 0.5W (13%)
- C. 0.75W (4%)
- D. W (6%)

Over three-quarters of candidates correctly applied the inverse square law of gravitation, recognising that weight decreases with the square of the distance from the Earth's centre.

#### Question 13

A point object *O* is placed in front of two plane mirrors *M₁* and *M₂* as shown.

*[diagram omitted]*

At which position(s) can an image be formed?

- A. P, Q and R (19%)
- *B. Q and R only (25%)
- C. R only (47%)
- D. P only (9%)

Candidates who chose option C identified only the image (*R*), which is formed by a single reflection and failed to identify the additional image (*Q*), which results from multiple reflections.

#### Question 14

*[diagram omitted]*

The figure shows three media *X*, *Y* and *Z* separated by parallel boundaries. A light ray travelling from *X* into *Y* then undergoes total internal reflection at the boundary between *Y* and *Z*. Arrange in descending order the speed of light in the respective media.

- A. X Y Z (19%)
- B. X Z Y (18%)
- C. Y Z X (13%)
- *D. Z X Y (50%)

Many candidates failed to relate the speed of light in various media to the bending of the light ray.

#### Question 18

In an experiment, monochromatic light is incident normally on a double slit and a diffraction grating placed in turn at the same position. The respective interference and diffraction patterns formed on the screen are observed. Which of the following statements about the patterns is/are correct?

(1) The fringe separation of the interference pattern is wider.
(2) The fringes of the diffraction pattern are sharper.
(3) The width of the whole diffraction pattern is wider.

- A. (1) only (18%)
- B. (2) only (22%)
- C. (1) and (3) only (23%)
- *D. (2) and (3) only (37%)

Candidates who chose options A and C seemingly did not realise that the pattern produced by a double slit is narrower than that produced by a diffraction grating.

#### Question 20

A source in water emits a beam of ultrasound towards the water-air interface as shown.

*[diagram omitted]*

Which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

- A. The frequency of the refracted beam is the same as that of the incident beam. (11%)
- B. The speed of the refracted beam becomes smaller. (16%)
- C. The refracted beam leaving the interface bends towards the normal. (27%)
- *D. If the angle of incidence is large enough, total internal reflection may occur. (46%)

Over half of the candidates incorrectly assumed that total internal reflection could occur when sound travels from water to air, without realising that sound waves travel faster in water.

#### Question 22

Three light conducting spheres are suspended by nylon threads. Any two of them are found to attract each other when they are brought close to each other. Which conclusion below is correct?

- A. All three spheres are charged. (8%)
- B. Only one sphere is charged. (7%)
- C. Only one sphere is uncharged and the other two carry like charges. (7%)
- *D. Only one sphere is uncharged and the other two carry unlike charges. (78%)

Nearly 80% of the candidates correctly recognised that the attraction between spheres can occur due to unlike charges and also between a neutral sphere and any charged sphere.

#### Question 27

The figure shows how lamps *L₁*, *L₂* and *L₃* are connected in the ring circuit of a house. There are some wrong connections in the circuit.

*[diagram omitted]*

If switches *S₂* and *S₃* are closed while switch *S₁* remains open, which of the lamps will light up?

- *A. L₂ only (33%)
- B. L₃ only (14%)
- C. L₂ and L₃ only (43%)
- D. none of them (10%)

Candidates who chose option B or C seemingly failed to recognise that there is no potential difference across *L₃* even if *S₃* is closed.

#### Question 30

An electric iron works at its rated power of 1100 W under a 50 Hz a.c. mains of root-mean-square voltage 220 V. What power will it deliver when working under a 100 Hz a.c. of root-mean-square voltage 110 V?

- *A. 275 W (48%)
- B. 778 W (12%)
- C. 550 W (22%)
- D. 1100 W (18%)

Nearly half of the candidates correctly applied the relation *P* ∝ *V_rms*², demonstrating that they were aware that power is independent of frequency.

## Paper 2

Paper 2 consists of four sections. Each section contains eight multiple-choice questions and one structured question which carries 10 marks. Section A contains questions on 'Astronomy and Space Science', Section B on the 'Atomic World', Section C on 'Energy and Use of Energy' and Section D on 'Medical Physics'. Candidates were required to attempt all questions in two of the four sections.

| Section | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Popularity | 17% | 66% | 86% | 31% |

### Section A : Astronomy and Space Science

#### Multiple-choice questions

Post-examination analysis revealed the following:

#### Question 1.5

Stars *X* and *Y* have the same surface temperature. The luminosities of *X* and *Y* are *L_X* = 121000 *L_S* and *L_Y* = 0.1 *L_S* respectively, where *L_S* is the luminosity of the Sun. Find *R_X* : *R_Y*, where *R_X* and *R_Y* are radii of *X* and *Y* respectively.

- A. 1210000 : 1 (10%)
- B. 1 : 1210000 (9%)
- *C. 1100 : 1 (67%)
- D. 1 : 1100 (14%)

Over two-thirds of candidates correctly applied the relation *L* ∝ *R*²*T*⁴, and successfully manipulated the ratios to obtain the correct answer.

#### Question 1.7

In the Hertzsprung-Russell (H-R) diagram, stars of the same apparent magnitude occupy

- A. the same horizontal line. (28%)
- B. the same vertical line. (14%)
- C. the same diagonal going from lower-right to upper-left. (27%)
- *D. arbitrary locations in the diagram. (31%)

Just under one-third of the candidates correctly recognised that stars of the same apparent magnitude can occupy arbitrary locations in the H-R diagram.

### Section B : Atomic World

#### Multiple-choice questions

Post-examination analysis revealed the following:

#### Question 2.1

In Thomson's plum pudding model, it is suggested that an atom is a positively charged sphere with electrons distributed evenly within it. Which of the following results of the α-scattering experiment implies that Thomson's model is incorrect?

(1) Most of the α particles pass straight through the thin gold foil.
(2) Some α particles are deflected by large angles.
(3) A few α particles are bounced back.

- A. (1) only (25%)
- B. (2) only (14%)
- C. (1) and (3) only (22%)
- *D. (2) and (3) only (39%)

Candidates who chose options A and C seemingly did not realise that most α particles passing straight through the foil is consistent with both the plum pudding model and the nuclear model, and therefore this does not invalidate Thomson's model.

#### Question 2.2

Which of the following about the Bohr atomic model is/are correct?

(1) Electrons are orbiting around the nucleus.
(2) The attractive force between the nucleus and the electron provides the centripetal force for the electron's circular motion.
(3) Photons are emitted when electrons jump from lower energy orbits to higher energy orbits.

- A. (2) only (4%)
- B. (3) only (5%)
- *C. (1) and (2) only (78%)
- D. (1) and (3) only (13%)

Nearly 80% of the candidates showed that they have a basic understanding of the motion of electrons in the Bohr atomic model.

#### Question 2.8

When a material is broken up into nanosized particles, which of the following statements is/are correct?

(1) Its chemical properties change but the physical properties remain unchanged.
(2) Its shape changes but the atomic arrangement remains unchanged.
(3) Its total surface area to volume ratio increases.

- A. (1) only (4%)
- *B. (3) only (39%)
- C. (2) and (3) only (44%)
- D. (1), (2) and (3) (13%)

Candidates who chose options C and D incorrectly assumed that the atomic arrangement remains unchanged, which is not necessarily true for nanomaterials.

##### Question 2

This question required candidates to solve problems involving energy levels of hydrogen atom and the photoelectric effect. The overall performance of candidates was fair. ### Section C : Energy and Use of Energy

#### Multiple-choice questions

Post-examination analysis revealed the following:

#### Question 3.1

In a room of dimensions *d* × *d* × *d*, identical point light sources, *P*, *Q*, *R* and *S*, are located at the four corners of the ceiling as shown. The luminous flux of each source is Φ.

*[diagram omitted]*

Find the illuminance around point *X* on the floor in the middle of the room. Neglect reflections from the walls of the room.

- A. Φ/(π*d*²) × (3/2)^(1/2) (19%)
- B. Φ/(π*d*²) × (3/2)^(3/2) (18%)
- C. Φ/(π*d*²) × (2/3)^(1/2) (23%)
- *D. Φ/(π*d*²) × (2/3)^(3/2) (40%)

About 40% of the candidates correctly calculated the illuminance at the centre of the floor by applying the inverse square law and summing the contributions from all four sources.

#### Question 3.4

When incident sunlight makes an angle of 20° to the normal of a solar panel, the electric power generated is 100 W.

*[diagram omitted]*

Assuming that the efficiency of the solar panel remains unchanged, find the electric power generated if the sunlight makes an angle of 30° to the normal of the panel and its intensity is reduced by 10%.

- A. 100 × (sin 30°)/(sin 20°) × 0.1 W (2%)
- B. 100 × (cos 30°)/(cos 20°) × 0.1 W (4%)
- C. 100 × (sin 30°)/(sin 20°) × 0.9 W (13%)
- *D. 100 × (cos 30°)/(cos 20°) × 0.9 W (81%)

Over 80% of candidates recognised that the effective power received by the panel is proportional to the cosine of the angle of incidence and the intensity of sunlight.

#### Question 3.8

What are the functions of pressurised water in the core of a pressurised water reactor?

(1) It acts as a moderator.
(2) It acts as a coolant.
(3) It turns into steam and drives the turbine.

- *A. (1) and (2) only (44%)
- B. (1) and (3) only (12%)
- C. (2) and (3) only (20%)
- D. (1), (2) and (3) (24%)

Candidates who chose options B, C and D wrongly assumed that the pressurised water turns into steam and drives the turbine. In fact, water in a separate secondary loop is turned into steam.

#### Structured questions

##### Question 3

This question assessed candidates' understanding of the energy aspects of lighting and air-conditioning systems. The overall performance of candidates was fair. Part (a)(i) was well attempted, although some confused the concepts of energy efficiency and efficacy. In (a)(ii), less than half recognised that green objects reflect green light and absorb red and blue light. In (b)(i), many applied the correct formula, but just under half substituted the correct values. Some failed to identify that each period lasted 12 hours rather than 24. Performance in (b)(ii) was satisfactory, though a few did not convert the energy unit into kWh. Part (b)(iii) was poorly answered; most did not identify that the minimum power requirement corresponded to the 8 a.m. - 8 p.m. period. The performance in (c) was fair. Some gave suggestions involving changes to the container's dimensions or materials.

#### Multiple-choice questions

Post-examination analysis revealed the following:

#### Question 4.3

An ultrasound transducer is set up as shown below.

#### Structured questions

##### Question 4

This question evaluated candidates' understanding and interpretation of curves of equal loudness. The overall performance was fair. In (a)(i), most candidates failed to recognise that sounds along the same curve are perceived as equally loud by the average person. In (a)(ii), about a third correctly defined the zero-phon curve as the threshold of hearing, while a few mistakenly argued that sounds at this level were inaudible. In (b), about half provided correct answers in both (i) and (ii), though some were unable to describe the curve's features accurately in (ii). The performance in (c) was satisfactory. In (c)(i), about two-thirds correctly determined that loudness and sound intensity levels are equal at 1 kHz. In (c)(ii), more than half successfully calculated the change in sound intensity levels across different frequencies given specific changes in loudness. Candidates performed poorly in (d)(i). Some struggled to apply the threshold of hearing (1 × 10⁻¹² W m⁻²) as the reference intensity for determining the ratio. Performance in (d)(ii) was fair. While some recognised that the curves would be raised, they failed to specify the corresponding part of the curve (right end).
